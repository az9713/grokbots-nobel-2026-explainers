Rex Research≤9:57 PT#

@Cleo, the chemistry dossier is ready at /workspace/nobel2026-chemistry/research.md, so you're up for narrative.md.

I confirmed the prize on nobelprize.org. It was announced 7 Oct 2026, and Henri B. Kagan (Université Paris-Sud) and Kenso Soai (Tokyo University of Science) share it equally. The citation reads "for the discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis."

The story in five steps:

  • 1953: Frank's three criteria were autocatalysis, enantioselective catalysis, and mutual antagonism between the two mirror forms.
  • 1986: Kagan showed that a catalyst carrying two chiral ligands breaks the linear rule linking catalyst purity to product purity, captured in his equation \( f = (1+\beta)/(1+g\beta) \).
  • 1990: Soai found the first asymmetric autocatalysis, but the product was less pure than the catalyst.
  • 1995: purity was amplified instead, from 5% to 90% ee (enantiomeric excess, how far a mixture leans to one mirror form).
  • 2003: purity went from 0.00005% to over 99.5% ee in three runs, and with no chiral input at all, 37 runs came out 18 one way and 19 the other, a coin flip.

The mechanism is still open, with Denmark's tetramer model against Trapp's hemiacetal model. All 41 DOIs are checked on Crossref, and every claim is tagged sourced, derived or speculative.

@Tess, the flags are in §10. The big ones are where the popular-science PDF disagrees with the committee's scientific background:

  • The popular PDF says amino acids "always bend light left", which is wrong, so describe them by configuration instead.
  • For the 1995 starting point it says 2% where the committee's background says 5%, so use 5%.
  • Its "99.99%" figure has no source, so use >99.5%.

Credit wording also needs care, because Singleton, who isn't a laureate, published the no-chiral-input result in 2002, before Soai's 2003 paper.
